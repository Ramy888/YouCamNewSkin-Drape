# Round 3: pivot the delivery model of "Drape"

Context: read brief.md, both round 2 answers (codex_r2.md, fable_r2.md), docs/app-flow.md (the current buyer-app flow) and
docs/derisk results summarized here:
- cloth-v4 transfers garment COLOUR faithfully and preserves the person, but the garment SHAPE varied between calls when the reference
  was a hand-recoloured image (ruffle vs cape). Real, clean colourway product shots are untested.
- Makeup VTO on a full-body try-on output may fail (needs face ≥100 px). Untested.
- Hard rule: no API spending without the owner's approval. About 994 units remain.

## The owner's challenge (two ideas)
1. **Seller side:** "Why build a store for buyers? Why not make the SELLER do the edits we need on the product they add, so the buyer gets the
   special experience?" Sellers would prepare the product: clean colourway shots, garment category, colour and palette tags, maybe
   pre-rendered assets, so the buyer's Drape experience is reliable and cheap.
2. **Extension:** "Besides an app, I want an extension for Amazon or any other store: one tap/click on the product starts the Drape experience for the buyer."

## Facts to respect
- The video must not show third-party trademarks or copyrighted material without permission (Amazon logo/UI on screen = risk). Judges must be
  able to test until Nov 20 (a load-unpacked Chrome extension from the repo is OK; Chrome Web Store review is optional/slow).
- The API key must stay server-side; the extension and app call our backend. One developer, Flutter + small backend, 1,000-unit budget.
- Arbitrary store product images are messy: on-model photos, collages, multiple people, lower-body-from-flat-shot is not allowed, and
  the colourway is often only a swatch. Clothes VTO needs a single garment, front-facing or worn by a single person.
- Judging: Tech Implementation (first tie-break), Design (complete product), Impact (real problem, real audience), Quality of Idea (non-obvious).

## What to produce (under 900 words)
1. Verdict on the **seller-side** idea: does it strengthen or weaken the hackathon entry and the real business? What exactly does the seller do,
   which YouCam calls run at seller time vs buyer time, and who pays the units?
2. Verdict on the **extension**: technically, how does one click work on Amazon or a generic store (DOM product-image extraction, colourway
   detection, category guess, failure modes)? What is the legal/ToS and trademark exposure for the demo video, and how do we avoid it?
3. Propose ONE **applicable final product architecture** that combines these: the surfaces (seller console / buyer extension / buyer app or
   web), the data each side provides, the API calls at each step with units, and what is in the 3-minute demo.
4. The MVP cut: what one developer must build by Nov 2, and what is shown as "roadmap" only.
5. The biggest single risk and the cheapest test for it (units; the owner must approve before running it).
