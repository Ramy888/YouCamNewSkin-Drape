Try & Buy as-is probably does not place top-3. It is coherent, shoppable, and track-aligned, but it reads like the expected answer: upload selfie, try outfit, get matching makeup, add to cart. Worse, Kostuma already owns the “outfit plus matching makeup basket” demo shape, just with Halloween IP. Try & Buy is more commercially credible, but judges watching 1–3 minutes may see “regional eCommerce wrapper around standard VTO,” not “from another planet.” The hijab gap also hurts: if modest fashion is the differentiator, the missing API support sits exactly where the promise lives.

### 1. Mirror Passport: One Face, Three Shopping Realities

Hook: The judge uploads one selfie and instantly sees three parallel selves: workday Cairo, wedding guest, and evening dinner, each with outfit, makeup, jewelry, and cart.

Non-obvious mechanism: Not “AI recommends products,” but “commerce as identity simulation.” The app sells complete social contexts. A look is not a SKU bundle; it is a persona-state with visual proof.

YouCam chain: Clothes VTO v4 for each outfit, Makeup VTO for each makeup map, Earrings/Necklace VTO for accessories. Optional Hair Color for one bolder persona. Cost: 3 outfits x 2 = 6, 3 makeup x 1 = 3, 3 jewelry x 1–2 = 3–6. Total ~12–15 units/session.

Commerce impact: Useful for MENA shoppers buying for high-context events: engagement visits, workwear, family dinners, Eid gatherings. The problem is not “what looks nice?” but “which version of me fits this situation without being too much or too little?” This can sell bundles credibly.

Riskiest assumption: Sequential VTO outputs stay visually clean when makeup/jewelry are applied after clothes. De-risk: test 3 chains on 2 selfies using Clothes + Makeup + Earrings/Necklace, ≤12 units.

3-day build: Yes. Use curated products and fixed personas. No complex AI needed. The video can look cinematic fast.

### 2. The Shade-Lock Fitting Room

Hook: A customer tries a dress, then the app proves which lipstick, blush, and jewelry colors harmonize with both their undertone and the garment, while showing bad matches rejected live.

Non-obvious mechanism: It turns VTO into a color-science proof, not a stylist. The “wow” is the app visibly locking a palette from face undertone plus garment color, then applying makeup and accessories that mathematically harmonize.

YouCam chain: Facial Color Tones for undertone = 20 units, Clothes VTO = 2, Makeup VTO with generated shades = 1, Earrings/Necklace = 1–2. Optional Fitzpatrick = 2 if Facial Color Tones is too expensive. Total: premium session ~24–25 units; cheap demo mode ~5–7 units.

Commerce impact: Big for beauty + fashion baskets, especially where foundation/lipstick mismatch and event outfits drive returns or hesitation. Audience: shoppers buying occasionwear who need makeup shades that survive different lighting and skin undertones.

Riskiest assumption: Facial Color Tones output is available and useful enough for visible palette logic. De-risk: run Facial Color Tones on one photo and compare generated warm/cool makeup results, ≤20–24 units depending one success.

3-day build: Yes, if scope is tight. Build a “palette engine” with deterministic rules, not ML. The cost is high, so demo should use one premium analysis then multiple low-cost try-ons.

### 3. Return-Proof Cart: The Fit Regret Simulator

Hook: The judge builds a cart, then the app shows “why you might return this” visually: neckline clashes with necklace, lipstick fights the fabric, shoes break the outfit, then fixes the cart in one tap.

Non-obvious mechanism: It reframes VTO from aspiration to regret prevention. Most shopping apps say “you’ll look great.” This one says “here are the three visual reasons you may hate this after delivery,” then uses VTO to resolve them.

YouCam chain: Clothes VTO for base outfit = 2, Makeup VTO for current/better makeup = 1–2, Necklace/Earrings/Shoes/Bag VTO alternatives = 2–6 depending categories. Total ~7–10 units/session.

Commerce impact: Extremely credible for eCommerce: reduce returns, increase basket confidence, sell substitutes. Audience: online fashion marketplaces, especially occasionwear and beauty bundles where items are bought separately and only clash after delivery.

Riskiest assumption: Shoe/bag/jewelry VTO outputs work well enough from one user photo to make “regret fixes” visible. De-risk: test one outfit plus two accessory swaps and one makeup swap, ≤8 units.

3-day build: Yes. The “regret engine” can be scripted with smart copy and rules: neckline vs necklace, warm/cool clash, formality mismatch. It will feel intelligent without needing real AI.

My pick: **Return-Proof Cart**.

It is the strongest hackathon concept because it is immediately different from Kostuma and from generic AI stylists. It has a sharp eCommerce reason to exist: fewer returns and higher basket confidence. It also creates a better video arc than Try & Buy: build cart, reveal hidden visual mistakes, fix them, checkout. That is memorable in 60 seconds.

Mirror Passport is more glamorous, but slightly more “styling app.” Shade-Lock is elegant, but Facial Color Tones is expensive and risks becoming color theory homework. Return-Proof Cart is weird enough to feel new, practical enough for judges to believe, and buildable by one developer in three focused days.
